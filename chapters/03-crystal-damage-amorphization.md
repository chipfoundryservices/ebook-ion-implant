# Chapter 3: Crystal Damage, Cascades & Amorphization

## 3.1 Collision Cascades & Frenkel Pairs
When an implanted ion transfers energy exceeding the silicon displacement threshold ($E_d \approx 15\text{ eV}$), a host silicon atom is ejected from its lattice site. This creates a vacancy and an interstitial atom—a **Frenkel pair**. The knocked-on silicon atom (primary knock-on atom, PKA) collides with neighboring atoms, generating a dense collision cascade of secondary displacements within $10^{-13}\text{ seconds}$.

## 3.2 Critical Amorphization Dose ($D_{\text{crit}}$)
When the deposited nuclear energy density exceeds approximately $5 \times 10^{23}\text{ eV/cm}^3$ (roughly $12\text{ eV/atom}$), individual damage cascades overlap completely, collapsing the crystalline silicon lattice into a continuous **amorphous silicon layer** ($a\text{-Si}$).

$$\Phi_{\text{crit}} \propto \frac{\Delta H_{\text{cryst}}}{S_n(E)}$$

- Heavy ions ($\text{As}^+, \text{Sb}^+, \text{Ge}^+$) amorphize at modest doses ($10^{13}\text{–}10^{14}\text{ cm}^{-2}$).
- Light ions ($\text{B}^+$) rarely form continuous amorphous layers at room temperature due to dynamic in-situ self-annealing, requiring cryogenic wafer chucking.

## 3.3 End-of-Range (EOR) Defects & $\{311\}$ Interstitial Clusters
Below the continuous amorphous layer lies the crystalline substrate populated by dense interstitial atoms that were displaced beyond the amorphous/crystalline ($a/c$) interface. Upon thermal annealing, these excess interstitials condense into $\{311\}$ rod-like defects and bounded dislocation loops. If these loops reside within the depletion region of a p-n junction, leakage current increases by $3\text{–}5$ orders of magnitude.
