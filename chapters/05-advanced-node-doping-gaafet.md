# Chapter 5: 3D Doping: FinFETs, GAAFETs & Plasma Immersion

## 5.1 The 3D Doping Challenge in Nanosheets
As transistor architectures transitioned from planar 2D to vertical FinFETs and horizontally stacked Gate-All-Around (GAAFET) nanosheets, line-of-sight beamline implantation faced acute physical constraints.

In a 3-tier nanosheet stack with channel spacing $<10\text{nm}$:
- Traditional ion beams suffer from shadowing by adjacent gate structures.
- Heavy ion bombardment damages the fragile $3\text{–}5\text{nm}$ thick silicon nanosheets, risking sheet bending and collapse.

## 5.2 Plasma Doping (PLAD / PIII)
Plasma Immersion Ion Implantation (PIII) places the wafer directly inside a dopant plasma (e.g., $\text{B}_2\text{H}_6/\text{He}$ or $\text{AsH}_3$). Pulsed negative high voltage ($-500\text{V to } -10\text{kV}$) applied to the wafer chuck collapses the plasma sheath, accelerating ions omnidirectionally into all 3D fin sidewalls simultaneously.

$$\text{Conformality} = \frac{\Phi_{\text{sidewall}}}{\Phi_{\text{top}}} \approx 0.85\text{–}0.95$$

## 5.3 Co-Implantation & In-Situ Doped Epitaxy
At the $2\text{nm}$ and $1.4\text{nm}$ nodes, physical ion implantation is strategically paired with in-situ doped selective epitaxy:
- **Embedded SiGe:B:** P-channel source/drain regions are grown epitaxially with in-situ Boron concentrations up to $3 \times 10^{20}\text{ atoms/cm}^3$.
- **Co-Implants:** Carbon ($C$) and Fluorine ($F$) are co-implanted to trap interstitials and suppress Boron deactivation during subsequent thermal processing.
